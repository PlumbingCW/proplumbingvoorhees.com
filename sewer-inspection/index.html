Camera inspection

Sewer Inspection in Voorhees, NJ

A sewer inspection is a recorded camera run through the pipe between your house and the connection, so you can see the material, slope and defects instead of guessing. It answers the question every repair estimate and every home purchase should start with.

Most visits are booked ahead. If sewage is already backing up, the 24/7 line takes urgent calls any hour.

Call (855) 575-2890

How the camera run works

The visit is short and does not dig up anything.

  1. 01

    Open the cleanout

    We find the access point, often outside near the foundation or in the basement, and remove the cap.

  2. 02

    Feed the camera

    A flexible rod carries a waterproof camera with lights into the pipe, pushed gently to the connection.

  3. 03

    Watch in real time

    We stop at each defect, note its distance from the cleanout and check which way the pipe slopes.

  4. 04

    Locate on the surface

    A transmitter on the camera head lets us mark the position above ground when a repair may follow.

  5. 05

    Hand over the footage

    You get the recording and a plain summary of what each finding means.

What the camera cannot do

A camera sees the inside of the pipe only up to where it can travel. A full blockage, a sharp turn or a collapse may stop it short. It does not test whether a line leaks under pressure, and it cannot judge the city main beyond the connection. We will say so in the summary instead of implying more.

Book an inspection if any of these apply

  • ✓You are buying a house built before 1980 and the seller has no pipe records
  • ✓The same drain has clogged twice in a season
  • ✓A tree has grown near the line since it was installed
  • ✓You smell sewer gas near a floor drain or in the yard
  • ✓A previous owner reports a repair but you have no paperwork
  • ✓You are planning a basement bathroom or an addition that will add load

Camera inspection versus other checks

Camera inspection

Direct view of the pipe interior.

  • Shows cracks, roots and slope
  • Produces a recording you keep
  • Needs a cleanout or a pulled toilet

Cable clearing alone

Opens the line without looking at it.

  • Fixes a simple blockage
  • Tells you nothing about the cause
  • Fine for a one-off clog only

If your line has not been checked and you have had more than one backup, cabling again without a look is the weaker choice.

What affects the price of an inspection

Line length

A short lateral takes less time than a long run to a far connection.

Access point

A clear exterior cleanout is quick. Entry through a toilet adds setup.

Cleaning first

A heavily blocked pipe may need clearing before the camera passes.

Locating

Marking a defect on the surface adds a step.

Report format

A recording plus a written summary takes more preparation than a verbal check.

Insurance note. Homeowner insurance may not pay for an inspection that is routine, though some policies cover the investigation after a covered loss, so check your policy. If you collect several quotes, confirm that each includes the recording and not only a verbal opinion.

After the footage, what next

A clean result means you can stop worrying and keep the video. A scaled but sound pipe may only need hydro jetting, and a damaged one moves to sewer line repair with the camera findings as the basis for the estimate.

If the report names a repeat clog in the main, sewer line cleaning can restore flow while you plan the next step. Homeowners in Somerdale book inspections for the same reasons.

A lake community inside Voorhees

Kirkwood is an unincorporated community in Voorhees Township, built up around Kirkwood Lake, an impoundment of the Cooper River. It grew as a bungalow community and a summer getaway for people from Philadelphia and Camden.

Many of the early houses were Sears-Roebuck catalogue homes, mostly built between 1915 and 1925. Kirkwood once had its own ZIP code. Today 08043 serves the Voorhees post office, and Kirkwood remains a valid mailing city name.

That history tells you less than you might expect. We have no verified figures on how many original bungalows remain or what has been renovated, so the sections below stick to general guidance.

Age is a clue, not a diagnosis

An older house can have its original pipes, a few updates or a complete replacement. A street's history does not tell you which. What you can do is look.

A visual check of exposed pipe in a basement or crawlspace shows the material and any crusting at the joints. A camera run through the main drain, which is what a sewer camera inspection does, shows the underground part nobody can see.

Repeated leaks in several places may lead to a conversation about repiping. A single leak rarely justifies replacing everything, so get the facts first.

What to look at in an older home

  • ✓The material of the visible supply pipe near the main shutoff
  • ✓Green or white crust at joints, valves and fittings
  • ✓Rust-colored water after the tap has sat unused
  • ✓Low pressure at the fixture farthest from the main
  • ✓Slow drains in more than one fixture at once
  • ✓Damp spots under sinks and around the water heater
  • ✓Any past repair records or permit papers you can find

Buying or renovating a lakeside bungalow

If you are buying or renovating, ask for a drain camera look and for the findings in writing. A small house may have tight spaces, so ask about access to the main shutoff and the cleanout before work starts. Our leak detection page explains how a leak is traced without opening walls.

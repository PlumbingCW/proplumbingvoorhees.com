Emergency Water Line Repair in Voorhees, NJ

A water line repair fixes the buried pipe that brings water from the main in the street to your meter and into the house. When it fails you may see a soaked lawn, a bill that jumped, weak pressure or no water at all, and our 24/7 line is open for it in Voorhees, NJ.

While you wait, close the main valve inside the house and note whether the meter dial still moves with every tap off.

Signs the leak is outside the house

Water lines fail underground, so the first clues are indirect. Tick what applies.

  • ✓A bill far above your normal use with nobody home more than usual
  • ✓A wet or sunken patch in the lawn or driveway when the weather has been dry
  • ✓Meter dial moving with every faucet and appliance off
  • ✓Pressure that dropped on every floor at once
  • ✓Hissing near the meter box or main valve
  • ✓Mud or water pooling around the curb or meter pit

Two or more ticks is enough to call. Don't wait for the lawn to flood.

Run the meter test yourself

  1. 01

    Turn everything off

    Close taps, stop the washer and dishwasher, and check that the ice maker is not filling.

  2. 02

    Read the meter

    Note the numbers or the small leak-indicator triangle.

  3. 03

    Wait twenty minutes

    Use no water at all during this time.

  4. 04

    Read again

    Movement means a leak somewhere. If the main valve inside closes and the dial still turns, the leak is on the buried side.

A toilet that runs on its own can also move the dial, so check those first. The test points to the line, not the exact spot.

Urgent: act first

No water, or water surfacing in the yard right now

If water is geysering in the yard, flooding a basement wall or the house has lost supply, treat it as urgent and call the 24/7 line. Same-day visits are available when schedules allow.

Tell us where the meter is, what you see and when it started, because a surfacing leak is usually near the line but not always on top of it.

How we find the exact break

Digging blind wastes money. We narrow the break with a pressure test of the line, acoustic listening at the meter and valves, and a pipe locator that traces the route underground.

Once the section is known, the dig is a small trench instead of a long one, which keeps your driveway, walk and landscaping intact as far as possible.

Spot repair, new trench or trenchless

Spot repair

Short section, sound pipe on either side.

  • Smallest dig
  • Fits a single break or a bad fitting
  • Not for a line with several failures

Full replacement in a trench

Old, corroded or lead and galvanized pipe.

  • Fresh line to the meter or house
  • Clear route and the most reliable result
  • More surface disturbance

Trenchless

Pull or burst a new line through the old path.

  • Less lawn and pavement damage
  • Needs entry points and a suitable path
  • Not right for every line or soil

We tell you which fits your line after locating it, and we say so when a simple repair beats a replacement.

Who owns what between the street and your house

The line has more than one owner along its length. Ask before you assume.

Main in the street

Belongs to the water utility, New Jersey American Water in Voorhees.

Utility service line

The utility owns part of the line, and where its section ends is a question for the utility.

Customer section

The rest of the line to the house. Ownership details vary, so confirm with the utility.

Inside piping

Everything after the main shutoff in the house is the owner's.

Lead, galvanized and the 2031 deadline in Voorhees

A 2021 New Jersey law requires water utilities to replace lead and galvanized service lines, including customer-owned ones, by 2031. New Jersey American Water says it will replace customer-owned lines found to be lead or galvanized, and customers can check their line material through the utility's lead facts page.

The utility also announced in 2022 that it would replace old cast iron mains, some dating to the 1950s, on ten Voorhees streets. If your line serves a house on one of those streets, ask whether work is planned before you pay for a replacement.

Low pressure is not always the water line

Weak flow at one tap is usually a clogged aerator or valve. Low pressure everywhere with a normal meter could be a failing regulator or partially closed main. Pressure lost together with a spinning meter points to the line.

How Do You Install an Expansion Tank on a Water Heater in Voorhees, NJ?

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Voorhees Pro Plumbing team

A thermal expansion tank is a small pressurized vessel, mounted on the cold supply line near a water heater, that absorbs the extra volume water takes up as it heats. Without it, a closed plumbing system has nowhere to send that volume, and pressure spikes every time the burner or element cycles.

This guide shows how the installation works, which steps are realistic for a careful homeowner, and which ones belong to a licensed plumber. It also covers the signs that your home may need a tank in the first place.

Why a closed system needs room for expanding water

Water expands as it warms. In an open system, that extra volume simply pushes back toward the street through the service line. Once a backflow preventer or a pressure reducing valve sits on your incoming line, the water can no longer escape that way.

The result is a pressure rise inside your pipes, fixtures and the tank itself. You may see the temperature and pressure relief valve drip after each heating cycle, hear a bang when a faucet closes, or watch a faucet washer wear out early. A correctly charged expansion tank gives that volume a place to go.

Signs your water heater may need an expansion tank

None of these signs proves the cause on its own. A plumber confirms with a pressure gauge on a hose bib.

A rule worth keeping

Never plug or cap a dripping relief valve to stop the drip. That valve is the last safety device on the heater, and a dripping one is a symptom, not the disease.

The installation sequence, start to finish

The order below is the one most plumbers follow. Read your tank's printed instructions too, because models differ.

  1. 01

    Shut off the fuel and water

    Switch the breaker off for an electric heater, or turn a gas heater's control to pilot or off. Close the cold water shutoff above the heater. If the shutoff does not close fully, stop here and call a plumber.

  2. 02

    Relieve pressure and drain a little

    Open a hot tap upstairs, then open the heater's drain valve with a hose to a floor drain until the cold line above the heater is empty. Water in the line will spill out of the cut otherwise.

  3. 03

    Set the tank's air charge

    Use a tire gauge on the tank's air valve. The charge should match the static water pressure you measured at a hose bib. Add air with a hand pump if it is low.

  4. 04

    Install a tee and mount the tank

    Fit a tee on the cold supply line within a short run of the heater. Support the tank with a bracket if it is heavy, because the fitting alone should not carry the weight. Use pipe thread sealant on threaded joints.

  5. 05

    Refill, purge air and check for leaks

    Open the cold shutoff slowly with a hot tap running so air escapes. Wait for steady flow, then restore fuel or power. Run a hand along every joint and watch for seepage over the first heating cycle.

Soldering or press fittings on the cold line add a second layer of skill. Sweat joints near a gas heater, wood framing or insulation can start a fire if you are not careful.

Do it yourself or hire a plumber

Both paths are legitimate. The right one depends on your pipe type, your comfort with fittings and the gas or electric supply.

Reasonable to try yourself

Threaded connections on an accessible cold line with a working shutoff.

  • Copper, CPVC or PEX with a screw-on adapter
  • Electric heater with the breaker off
  • Gauge, air pump and thread sealant on hand
  • No sign of corrosion at the existing fittings

Better to call a plumber

Anything that touches gas, soldering or a stuck valve.

  • Gas heater with a draft hood or flue near the work
  • Corroded unions or a shutoff valve that will not close
  • Sweat joints in a tight space
  • Pressure above normal on your gauge, which may need a pressure reducing valve

A plumber installing a heater through water heater installation usually adds the tank in the same visit, and a repair call through water heater repair can diagnose a dripping relief valve before you buy parts.

What Voorhees homeowners should know about permits

According to the New Jersey Department of Community Affairs Construction Code Communicator, replacing an existing water heater with one of like capacity is minor work. Minor work may begin once the enforcing agency has been notified, and plumbing work must be done by persons licensed to perform it.

Adding an expansion tank is usually part of that replacement visit. If you are adding one alone, permit rules vary, so ask the Voorhees Construction Department before you start.

Source type: NJ DCA Construction Code Communicator

Mistakes that cause callbacks

These are the errors plumbers meet most often when they fix a homeowner's first attempt.

  • ✓Setting the air charge after the tank is already full of water, which gives a false reading
  • ✓Mounting the tank hanging downward without a support strap
  • ✓Skipping the pressure gauge and guessing the charge
  • ✓Leaving air trapped in the line so the first shower spits and knocks
  • ✓Installing on the hot side instead of the cold side
  • ✓Forgetting that a tank with a failed bladder will look fine from outside

When to call the 24/7 line instead

Stop and make the call if you smell gas, see rust-colored water from the heater, or find a puddle under the tank. For gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

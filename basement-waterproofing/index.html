Basement Waterproofing in Voorhees, NJ

Basement waterproofing is the set of drainage, sealing and pumping measures that keep groundwater and surface water out of a basement or crawlspace. In Voorhees, NJ, the first job is working out whether the water is coming from the ground, a roof or a plumbing leak, because each has a different fix.

We focus on the plumbing side: sump pits, drain lines, discharge pipes and leaks. If water is entering right now, call the 24/7 line at (855) 575-2890.

Start by finding where the water comes from

Painting a wall or running a dehumidifier hides a symptom. Water that comes from outside needs a path out, and water from a pipe needs the pipe fixed.

A simple look at timing helps. Water that shows up only after rain points to groundwater or runoff. Water that appears on a dry day points to a supply line, a drain or a water heater.

What to do before we arrive

If water is coming in as you read this, these steps limit damage while you wait.

  • ✓Stay out of standing water near outlets. Turn off power to the basement at the breaker only if you can reach the panel without wading.
  • ✓Lift what you can. Move boxes, rugs and furniture onto blocks or higher shelves, starting with anything electrical or paper.
  • ✓Check the sump pit. If a pump is installed, see whether it is running and whether the discharge line outside is open and clear.
  • ✓Photograph the water line and the entry points. Dated photos help the assessment and any insurance conversation.

Three ways to keep a basement dry

Each addresses a different failure.

Outside: grade, gutters, downspouts

Move rain away from the foundation first.

  • Lowest cost
  • Does nothing for a high water table
  • Call 811 before digging outdoors

Inside: perimeter drain and sump

Collect water at the footing and pump it out.

  • Works for most seepage
  • Needs a reliable pump, see sump pump
  • Requires cutting the floor

Exterior excavation and membrane

Dig down, seal and drain the foundation wall.

  • Most disruptive
  • Highest cost
  • Mainly for failing walls and persistent leaks

Interior drains handle water that reaches the basement. Outside work stops water before it arrives. Most houses need a mix.

Crawlspaces and under-house leaks

A crawlspace can flood from rain runoff or from a burst or leaking pipe, and the two look similar until you check for steady flow. A pipe split under the house often leaves a constant wet spot and a rising water bill, while runoff comes and goes with the weather.

Supply lines in unheated crawlspaces are also at risk in freezes. Heat tape, insulation and a working shutoff valve are cheaper than repairing a split pipe.

If you suspect a pipe, see leak detection for how we locate it.

What you can do this week for free

  • ✓Clean gutters and extend downspouts several feet from the foundation.
  • ✓Make sure soil slopes away from the walls rather than toward them.
  • ✓Check window wells for debris and standing water.
  • ✓Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit.
  • ✓Look for drips under sinks, around the water heater and at the main shutoff.
  • ✓Note when water appears: during rain, after a shower, or at random.

When NOT to waterproof

If the water comes from a pipe, waterproofing will not help. A leak needs a plumber, and sealing over it can trap moisture and cause mold.

What drives the cost of waterproofing

Length of drain or wall treated

More linear feet mean more cutting and more material.

Type of floor and wall

Poured concrete, block and finished surfaces behave differently.

Pump and backup

A sump pit, pump, check valve and backup system add equipment.

Exterior access

Decks, sidewalks and landscaping complicate digging.

Finish and cleanup

Patching concrete and restoring finished areas add labor.

Insurance note. Homeowner insurance may exclude groundwater seepage, and flood coverage is usually separate, so check your policy. Ask for each step to be itemized so you can compare quotes fairly.

Common questions

Waterproofing is the whole system. A sump pump is the part that removes collected water from a pit. Many jobs need both.

Paint can slow moisture through masonry but cannot stop pressure from groundwater. It often peels when real water arrives.

Seepage follows rain and shows along walls and floor edges. Leaks come from a pipe, appliance or drain and appear on dry days. A water meter test helps confirm a pipe leak.

The county plan rates township flood hazard High and lists lake and road flooding spots, but it does not count basements. Your own lot, grade and past water are the best guide.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Voorhees Construction Department before starting. Plumbing work must be done by licensed persons.

Shut off the main water, then call. A burst line needs repair, not drainage. Our team can locate the break and fix it.

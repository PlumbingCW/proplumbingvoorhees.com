Homemade Solutions for a Clogged Drain in Voorhees, NJ

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Voorhees Pro Plumbing team

A homemade drain solution is any fix you can do with items already in the house, such as boiling water, a plunger, baking soda with vinegar or a hand-turned drain snake. These work on soft, local clogs near the fixture. They do not work on deep blockages, tree roots or a backed-up main line.

The goal here is to help you try the safe, cheap steps in the right order and to know the point where another attempt just makes the mess bigger.

Fix it yourself, try tools, or call now

Start by reading how widespread the problem is. One slow fixture and a house-wide backup are very different jobs.

Home fix

One slow sink, tub or shower

Water drains slowly but does drain. The clog is almost always hair, soap scum or grease in the trap or just beyond it.

Tools or call

One fixture fully blocked

A plunger and a hand auger may clear it. If two honest attempts fail, the clog is likely deeper than you can reach.

Call now

Several fixtures slow, gurgling, or water in a floor drain

That points to the main line. Stop using water and do not keep pouring products down.

A safe order to try things

Work from gentlest to most hands-on. Stop as soon as the water moves freely.

  1. 01

    Remove what you can see

    Pull the stopper or strainer and clear hair and gunk with a gloved hand or a bent wire. In a bathroom sink, unscrewing the pop-up lever and lifting the stopper often shows the whole problem.

  2. 02

    Flush with hot tap water

    Run hot water from the tap for a minute or two. For a kitchen grease clog, this can melt a soft plug. Do not pour boiling water into a toilet or onto cracked porcelain, and be careful with PVC traps.

  3. 03

    Plunge correctly

    Use a flat-bottomed cup plunger on sinks and tubs. Block the overflow opening with a wet rag, make a seal, and pump firmly ten times. A flange plunger is the right tool for toilets.

  4. 04

    Try baking soda and vinegar

    Pour half a cup of baking soda down the drain, follow with half a cup of white vinegar, and cover the opening for fifteen minutes. Then flush with hot water. It helps with light residue, not with solid clogs.

  5. 05

    Use a hand auger

    Feed the cable in until it stops, crank to hook the clog, and pull it back out slowly. Wear gloves. If the cable runs out of length without reaching the end, the clog is farther than a household tool reaches.

If those five steps did not work, the clog is past the trap or in a branch line. Professional drain cleaning uses longer, stronger cables with cutting heads, and that is the next honest step.

Why what you pour matters in Voorhees, NJ

In Voorhees, the municipality collects sewage and conveys it to the Camden County Municipal Utilities Authority. That means what leaves your drain does not just disappear into your yard. It enters a public system shared with your neighbors.

Grease, wipes and harsh chemicals are not only a problem for your own pipes. They can contribute to blockages and equipment problems farther downstream. Treat the drain like a shared line, because it is one.

Safety stops before you pour or plunge

These are the places where homemade fixes injure people or damage plumbing.

  • ✓Never mix bleach, ammonia or commercial drain cleaners. The fumes can be dangerous.
  • ✓Do not plunge or snake a drain that has chemical cleaner sitting in it. The splashback can burn skin and eyes.
  • ✓Wear gloves and eye protection when you clean a trap or run an auger.
  • ✓Do not use a hand auger past a gurgling main or into a toilet. A scratched bowl or damaged trap is a bigger bill than the clog.
  • ✓If sewage is coming up from a floor drain, keep children and pets away and call. That water is contaminated.

Homemade fix, cable or jet: how they differ

Homemade

Hot water, plunger, baking soda, hand auger.

  • Fine for soft, local clogs
  • No cost beyond what you own
  • Cannot clear roots or heavy buildup

Cable (snaking)

A powered cable bores through or hooks the blockage.

  • Clears most household clogs
  • Leaves grease film on pipe walls
  • Reaches branch and main lines

Hydro jetting

High-pressure water scours the inside of the pipe.

  • Cleans walls, not just the clog
  • Good for grease and scale
  • Pipe must be sound enough for it

When grease or scale keeps bringing a clog back, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the blockage.

Why the clog keeps coming back

A drain that clears for a week and slows down again usually has a buildup problem, not a one-time blockage. Grease layers, mineral scale and a rough pipe wall all catch new debris. Chemical products tend to punch a channel through the middle and leave the buildup behind.

A pipe that is cracked, sagging or invaded by roots will also clog repeatedly. If your cable keeps hitting the same spot, ask for a camera look. Neighbors in Somerdale and West Berlin see the same pattern in older lines.

A good rule: two home attempts, then stop. A third or fourth round of products and plunging rarely works, and it can push the clog deeper or leave caustic liquid standing in the pipe.

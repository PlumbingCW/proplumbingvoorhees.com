Water Heater Igniter Replacement Cost Factors in Voorhees, NJ

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Voorhees Pro Plumbing team

A water heater igniter is the part that lights the gas burner, either with a glowing hot surface element or with a spark. When it fails, the burner never fires and you get lukewarm or cold water. The part is small, but the final price depends on the heater, the access and what else the technician finds.

This guide explains the cost drivers instead of quoting a number, because the real figure depends on your heater. It also starts with the one situation where you should not be reading at all.

Urgent: act first

If you smell gas, leave first

A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater is not a cost question. Do not light anything, flip switches or use a phone inside. Leave the building, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Both South Jersey Gas and PSE&G publish leave-first guidance for gas odors. Do not go back inside to look for the cause.

  • 1Leave the building and take everyone with you.
  • 2Call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • 3Stay out until the utility or fire department says it is safe.
  • 4Call a plumber only after the gas has been checked and cleared.

How the igniter fits into the heater

Newer gas tank heaters and many tankless units use an electronic igniter. A control board sends power to a hot surface element or a spark electrode. A flame sensor then confirms the burner lit. If the igniter is weak or cracked, the board tries several times and then locks out.

Older tanks use a standing pilot that is lit by hand or with a push-button piezo. That pilot assembly has a thermocouple, and it fails in a different way. Which system you have matters, because the parts and labor differ.

What sets the price of an igniter job

No two igniter jobs cost the same, and a flat number on a web page can hide the details that move the bill.

Heater type and brand

Tank and tankless units use different igniters, and some brands use sealed assemblies that cost more than a loose element.

Part availability

A common igniter may be on the truck. A part for a discontinued or specialized unit may need ordering, which can add a second visit.

Diagnosis time

The same symptom can come from the igniter, the flame sensor, the gas valve, venting or a control board. Testing takes time, and it protects you from replacing the wrong part.

Access and location

A heater in a tight closet, attic or crawlspace takes longer to work on than one in an open basement.

Related condition

Sediment, corrosion, a flue problem or a leaking tank can change the answer from repair to replacement.

Timing

A scheduled appointment generally costs less than an after-hours emergency call.

Insurance note. Homeowner insurance may not cover a worn igniter because it is normal wear, so check your policy and any home warranty. Ask any plumber for a written, itemized price that separates the part, the labor and the diagnosis.

Repair the igniter or replace the heater

An igniter is usually a worthwhile repair on a heater that is otherwise sound. It is a poor investment on a tank that is failing.

Repair makes sense when

The tank is not leaking, has no heavy corrosion and was working well until the igniter stopped.

  • Heater is in the middle of its service life
  • Part is available
  • No other faults found

Replace makes sense when

The tank is leaking, rusting at the base, or already needed other repairs.

  • Tank is near the end of its life
  • Repair cost is a large share of a new unit
  • You want a different capacity or a tankless model

If the cost of the repair is a big share of a new heater, look at water heater replacement as a decision instead of paying to patch an old tank.

Gas safety in Voorhees, NJ homes

A gas appliance repair is one of the few household jobs where a mistake can hurt people. In Voorhees, the right first call for any gas smell is the gas utility or 911, not a plumber and not a neighbor.

Both South Jersey Gas and PSE&G tell customers to leave the building before calling. Look at your gas bill to confirm who serves your address, and keep that number somewhere you can find it.

How to compare igniter quotes

  • ✓Does the quote name the heater model and the part number?
  • ✓Is the diagnosis fee separate from the repair, and is it credited if you proceed?
  • ✓Will the plumber test the flame sensor, gas valve and venting too?
  • ✓Is the work done by a licensed plumber?
  • ✓Does the quote say what happens if another fault is found?
  • ✓Will the plumber leave the old part so you can see it?

What a licensed plumber will and will not do

You can safely check the obvious things at home: the thermostat setting, the breaker, the switch on the gas valve and whether the vent is blocked by stored items. Do not remove the burner access panel to work on a lit gas heater or rewire the control board.

A licensed plumber tests gas pressure, checks combustion and venting, replaces the igniter and confirms a clean burn. If the unit is old enough that the part is hard to find, they should tell you plainly. Our water heater repair visits start with this diagnosis for exactly that reason.

Common questions

Clicking usually means the spark system is trying. Common causes are a dirty or misaligned electrode, a gas supply that is shut off, or a failing igniter. A technician can test each part.

On some models the part is accessible, but the job involves gas and sealed combustion parts. If you are not trained, a mistake can leak gas or cause a poor burn. Most homeowners should leave this to a licensed plumber.

A straightforward job with the part on hand is often completed in one visit. A hard-to-reach heater or a part that needs ordering will take longer.

Replacing a part on an existing heater is a repair, not a new installation. Permit rules vary, so ask the Voorhees Construction Department if you are unsure.

A technician should test it with a meter and check the flame sensor and gas valve before replacing anything. Ask for the test results, not just the part.

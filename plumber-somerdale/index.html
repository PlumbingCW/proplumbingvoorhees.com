Somerdale, NJ: the Cooper River runs through the problem

The Camden County hazard mitigation plan (draft) says the Cooper River divides Somerdale from Voorhees. It lists homes along Osage Avenue and Hartner Avenue backing onto the river, and homes and businesses along Kennedy, Maiden, Parkview, Cedar and Juniper Avenues that also back onto it.

The same plan names the Somerdale Road bridge over the Cooper River as a repeat problem spot. It records flooding up to 36 inches on the 200 block of West Maiden Lane, and parts of Route 30 near the 500 block of South White Horse Pike that flood. It also notes that storm-drain capacity is limited, so heavy rain can pond on a street even away from the river.

If your lot backs onto the river, a groundwater-fed basement is a normal risk, not a freak event.

Spring and summer storms: the first hour

Thunderstorms push the river up fast. This is the order that protects the most.

  1. 01

    Check the sump pit

    If the water is within a few inches of the top, make sure the pump is plugged in and the float moves freely.

  2. 02

    Look at the discharge

    The outlet pipe should carry water well away from the house and not ice up or be blocked by leaves.

  3. 03

    Move what can float

    Lift boxes, the dryer and electronics off the floor. A water heater that sits in the water is a hazard, so tell us about it.

  4. 04

    Call before it peaks

    A pump that fails at 2 a.m. is much harder to fix once the floor is wet. Our sump pump crew handles after-hours calls.

A pump that runs constantly is not always healthy. It may be short-cycling because of a stuck float or a failed check valve.

Why a built-out borough still gets wet

The county annex describes Somerdale as about 97 percent built out, mostly through redevelopment. That leaves few open lots to soak up rain, so water goes to the storm drains, the basements or the river.

Redevelopment can leave old plumbing tied to new work, and nobody can see that from the street. If your drain cleaning needs repeat, ask for a camera look.

Kennedy Boulevard, where Borough Hall sits, and the parks at Kennedy Boulevard Field, Perry Memorial Park and Red Fox Run give a sense of how close open ground and homes sit to the Cooper River corridor.

Before we arrive at your Somerdale home

Do these while the emergency crew is on the way.

Step 1

Close the main valve

If the water comes from a pipe, turn the main valve clockwise. For river water or sewage, this step will not stop it, so skip ahead.

Step 2

Stay away from electrics

Do not step into standing water to reach a panel. If water is at outlet height, leave the room and tell us.

Step 3

Clear a path

Move furniture from the basement door and the sump pit so the crew can work.

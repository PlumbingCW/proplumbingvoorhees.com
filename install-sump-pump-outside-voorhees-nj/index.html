How to Install a Sump Pump and Its Outside Discharge in Voorhees, NJ

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Voorhees Pro Plumbing team

A sump pump is a submersible pump that sits in a pit in the lowest part of the basement or crawlspace and pushes groundwater out of the house before it can flood the floor. The outside part of the system is the discharge line that carries that water through the wall and away from the foundation. Both halves have to be right or the pump just recycles water back toward the house.

This guide covers the layout, the digging safety step that people skip, the discharge rules of thumb and a clear list of what is reasonable to do yourself and what is not.

The system at a glance

Pit

A lined basin set below the slab so water collects in it

Pump

A submersible pump with a float switch or a vertical float

Check valve

Stops water in the line from falling back into the pit

Discharge pipe

Solid pipe through the rim joist or wall to the outside

Outlet

A spot that carries water well away from the foundation

Power

A GFCI-protected outlet, ideally on its own circuit

Call 811 before any digging in Voorhees, NJ

Running a discharge line to a drain, a dry well or a lower part of the yard usually means digging a trench. In New Jersey, you must contact NJ One Call by dialing 811 at least three business days before you dig. Utilities will mark buried lines.

Within two feet of a marked line, dig by hand. This applies to homeowners too, and it matters because gas, water and electric lines can be shallower than people expect.

Source type: NJ One Call

The installation sequence

These steps describe a typical install in a basement with a slab. Each house is different.

  1. 01

    Pick the low spot

    Find the lowest corner or where water enters first. Check for buried pipes before you cut concrete.

  2. 02

    Break the slab and dig the pit

    Cut a clean opening with a concrete saw, dig a hole sized to the basin, and set it level on a bed of gravel.

  3. 03

    Set the pump and check valve

    Place the pump on a firm base so the float moves freely, thread the discharge pipe, and install the check valve above the pump.

  4. 04

    Run the discharge through the wall

    Drill through the rim joist or foundation wall at a height that leaves a clean exit, then seal the opening around the pipe.

  5. 05

    Extend the line outdoors

    Connect solid pipe to a route that slopes away from the house. Mark the line, call 811 first, and keep any trench clear of utilities.

Test the system by filling the pit with a hose, confirming the float turns the pump on and off and watching the outside outlet for steady flow.

Do it yourself or hire a plumber

Reasonable DIY

Replacing a pump in an existing pit or extending a discharge hose to a safer spot.

  • Pump swap with the power off
  • Adding a splash block
  • Cleaning the pit and checking the float

Better with a plumber

New pit, new discharge route or any change that touches the foundation.

  • Breaking concrete safely
  • Sizing the pump and line
  • Placing the discharge correctly

A basic sump pump swap is often manageable. A new pit and discharge route is a bigger job, and permit rules vary, so ask the Voorhees Construction Department before starting.

How urgent is your water problem?

Plan it

Damp walls or an occasional damp floor

You have time to plan a pit and choose a discharge route.

Soon

Water after heavy rain

Get the pump in before the next storm. Move valuables up off the floor.

Now

Standing water, a failed pump or water near the electrical panel

Stay out of the water and call a plumber. Electricity and water together are a shock hazard.

Safety and mistakes to avoid

Never work on a pump while it is plugged in, and never stand in water to do it. Use a GFCI outlet, and do not run extension cords across a wet floor.

Common mistakes include discharging too close to the foundation, forgetting the check valve, and burying a line too shallow for winter. A long discharge hose that freezes will make the pump run and push nothing.

Why flooding history matters in Voorhees

The Camden County hazard plan ranks the township's flood hazard as High and names Burnt Mill Road and Somerdale Road among the places with flooding problems. Hurricane Irene in 2011 was a declared disaster. Dry months do not predict the next heavy storm.

Homeowners in nearby Lindenwold and Gibbsboro should ask the same question: is a basement waterproofing system needed on top of a pump? If water enters through the walls as well as the floor, a pump alone may not be enough.

Before you buy parts

  • ✓Do you know where water enters and how often?
  • ✓Is there a GFCI outlet within reach of the pit?
  • ✓Have you checked a backup option for power loss, such as a battery system?
  • ✓Do you have a discharge route that slopes away and does not cross a neighbor's yard?
  • ✓Have you called 811 if any digging is planned?

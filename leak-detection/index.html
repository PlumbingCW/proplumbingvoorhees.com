Emergency Leak Detection in Voorhees, NJ

Water you cannot see is still damaging the house, so a stain that is spreading or a meter that will not stop is worth a call to our 24/7 line. While you wait, shut the main valve if the leak is active and switch off power to any wet area.

Leak detection is the work of locating a hidden water leak, in a wall, a slab, a ceiling or an underground line, using meters, acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging so only the damaged spot is opened. It replaces demolition by guesswork.

The meter test: is water leaking right now

This takes ten minutes and tells you whether the leak is real.

  • ✓Turn off everythingClose every faucet, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure nobody flushes.
  • ✓Read the meterFind the low-flow dial or the small triangle that spins with even a drip, and note its position.
  • ✓Wait fifteen minutesDo not use any water. Check the dial again.
  • ✓CompareIf it moved, water is leaving your system. If it did not, the leak may be intermittent or the high bill comes from usage.
  • ✓Close the main valve and check once moreIf the meter still moves, the leak is between the meter and your shutoff, which is the service line.

A running toilet is the most common cause of a moving meter, so check the flapper before you assume the worst.

Your water meter, your bill and your service line

New Jersey American Water supplies Voorhees, and its meter is the best leak detector you own. A jump in the usage on your bill with no change in habits is a reason to run the meter test above and then call.

New Jersey law adopted in 2021 requires lead and galvanized service lines to be replaced by 2031, and the utility says it will replace customer-owned lines it finds to be lead or galvanized. If we find a failing service line, we will tell you what it is made of so you can ask the utility before you pay for a repair.

Active leak right now: first steps

An actively dripping ceiling or a water heater leaking in the garage is an emergency, and the 24/7 line is the fastest way to a plumber. The first ten minutes decide how much damage you get.

  • 1Shut off the waterClose the main valve, or the cold supply to the water heater if that is the source.
  • 2Cut power near waterTurn off breakers for wet rooms. Never touch a wet outlet.
  • 3Catch and containPut buckets under drips and move belongings out of the path.
  • 4Photograph the damageYou will want pictures if you file a claim.
  • 5Call usWe offer same-day scheduling when available and can talk you through the shutoff.

If you smell gas, leave the building first and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not look for the source yourself.

Non-invasive detection versus opening the wall

Detection first

Locate, then open one small hole.

  • Repairs a drywall patch, not a whole room
  • Costs a detection fee up front
  • Works on slabs and buried lines

Open and search

Remove material until the leak shows.

  • Faster for an obvious pipe
  • Risks damage to good wall and flooring
  • Poor choice for a slab or a yard

The honest limit is that tools narrow a leak to a few feet, not to an inch. Some jobs still need a test hole to confirm.

Repair after detection

Fixing the leak is separate from finding it.

Insurance note. Homeowner insurance may cover the sudden damage a leak causes but not the pipe repair or a long-term seep, so check your policy and keep your photos. When comparing quotes, ask whether the detection fee is credited if you hire the same company for the repair.

What happens once we find it

If the leak is in a supply line that has failed, the fix may be a short splice or a section replacement through burst pipe repair. Piping may be replaced to repair a leak as ordinary maintenance under New Jersey rules, which usually keeps paperwork simple.

A leak in a drain or sewer line is a different job, covered by sewer line repair. If a pipe shows widespread pitting, we will tell you plainly and discuss water line repair or a larger plan.

Homes and businesses we visit

We look for leaks in single-family houses, apartment buildings, offices and shops, including floors with drains that overflow when a fixture leaks upstairs. We also take calls from Gibbsboro and the other towns around Voorhees.

Tell us at booking whether you have a slab, a crawlspace or a basement, and whether a sprinkler system is on the property.

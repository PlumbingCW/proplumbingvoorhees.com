Before we arrive at your Gibbsboro home

These steps limit damage while the emergency crew is on the way.

Step 1

Shut the main water valve

Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, close the valve at the water meter.

Step 2

Kill power near standing water

If the water is near outlets, the panel or the water heater, switch off those breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise stay out and tell us.

Step 3

Clear the area

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. Keep children and pets out of a flooded basement, because water can hide a live circuit.

Step 4

Call the 24/7 line

Ring (855) 575-2890, give your street and tell us where the water is coming from. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Take two photos of the damage before you move anything. You may want them for your insurance claim.

Water in the basement: pump problem or drain problem?

Both look like a wet floor. The fix is different, so a quick check saves a wasted trip.

Pump or groundwater problem

Water arrives during or after rain and stands near the sump pit or along a wall.

  • Pit fills faster than the pump empties it
  • Pump hums but does not move water, or never starts
  • Fix: new float switch, check valve or a larger pump

Drain or sewer problem

Water arrives with no rain, often with a smell, and comes up at a floor drain or a low fixture.

  • Gurgling when you run a sink or washer
  • Water backs up in the lowest drain first
  • Fix: cable or hydro jetting, then a camera look

A cheap fix is not always the right one. If the pump is old and the pit fills often, a replacement costs less than another flooded basement.

After a storm: what to check in your Gibbsboro house

Walk through these within a day of heavy rain, even if nothing looks wrong.

  • ✓Sump pitPour a bucket of water in and watch the pump start and shut off.
  • ✓Discharge lineConfirm water leaves the house and does not drain back to the foundation.
  • ✓Floor drainsRun a faucet and watch for slow draining or gurgling.
  • ✓Water heater baseLook for rust, wetness or a stain on the floor.
  • ✓MeterWith all fixtures off, see if the meter dial still moves. If it does, you may have a hidden leak.

If anything fails the check, call the 24/7 emergency line instead of waiting for the next storm.

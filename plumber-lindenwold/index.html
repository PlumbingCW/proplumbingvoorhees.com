Renting in Lindenwold: who calls the plumber

Only about 30 percent of Lindenwold homes are lived in by their owners, according to Census QuickFacts. That means most plumbing problems here involve a tenant, a landlord or a property manager, and sometimes all three. Before anyone grabs a wrench, settle who is responsible.

In general a landlord covers supply lines, drains and the water heater that came with the building. A tenant covers what they caused, like a grease clog or a flushed toy. Leases and state rules vary, so read yours.

One honest trade-off: calling the landlord first is the right move for a slow drain, but it is the wrong move for a pipe that is spraying. If water is pouring out and nobody answers, shut the water off and call us. A leak in one unit soaks the ones below it.

Before we arrive: a burst pipe or active leak

Four things to do in the first few minutes, while the emergency plumber is on the way.

Step 1

Shut the main water valve

Turn it clockwise until it stops. In an apartment, look under the sink or in a utility closet for the unit's own valve.

Step 2

Keep power away from water

Do not step into standing water near outlets or appliances. Switch off the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Step 3

Clear the area

Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off the floor. Keep children and pets out of the wet room.

Step 4

Call and say who owns the unit

Dial (855) 575-2890. Tell us if you rent, so we know who needs the repair details.

If you ever smell gas, leave the building first and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

Lindenwold water, sewer and flooded roads

Lindenwold is a franchise customer of New Jersey American Water, and the borough runs its own Sewer Department. A blocked sewer main is a borough matter, while a blocked branch inside your walls is a plumber matter. If you cannot tell where the line changes hands, ask the borough Sewer Department before paying for digging.

The Camden County hazard mitigation plan (draft) lists undersized culverts at Berlin Road, Chews Landing Road and the White Horse Pike that flood repeatedly. It also notes that a borough sewer pump station sits in the 100-year flood zone. If a floor drain gurgles during a storm, the sewer may be overloaded, so stop using water and call.

Clear it yourself, or send for a plumber

Honest advice on a clogged drain, since a bad DIY try can turn a small job into a flood.

Try it yourself first

For one slow sink or tub with no smell and no other drains affected.

  • Plunge with the overflow blocked
  • Use a hand auger, not harsh chemicals
  • Stop if water rises somewhere else

Call a licensed plumber

For anything that repeats or reaches the floor.

  • Sewage in a tub or floor drain
  • Two or more drains slow at once
  • A clog that returns within weeks

Drain chemicals can burn skin and damage old pipe. They also make a later cable job unsafe for whoever runs it.

Permits, licenses and water heaters

New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by licensed persons. Replacing a water heater with one of like capacity counts as minor work under the state construction code, which means the enforcing agency must be notified before work starts. Plumbing permits in Lindenwold are handled by the borough, so ask the office that handles construction permits about your specific job.

Landlords sometimes ask tenants to handle a tank swap themselves. Do not. An unlicensed install can void the work and put a gas or electric heater at risk. A planned swap is covered on our water heater replacement page.

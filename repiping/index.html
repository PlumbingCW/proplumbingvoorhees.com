Pipe replacement

Repiping in Voorhees, NJ

Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes inside a building with new copper or PEX, either the whole house or the failing section. It is the answer when patches stop holding, not the first thing to try.

If a pipe is leaking right now, shut the main and call our 24/7 line. For everything else, we plan the work around your schedule.

What to do before we arrive

  • ✓Find your main shutoff. Know where the main water valve is and that it turns, because a failing old line can let go without warning.
  • ✓Photograph stains and low-pressure spots. Note which fixtures run weak and where ceiling or wall stains show.
  • ✓Do not use chemical cleaners on corroded lines. They add stress to thin pipe walls and can splash during a repair.
  • ✓Clear access. Empty the cabinet under the kitchen sink and clear the path to the water heater and meter.

Why pipes wear out before the house does

Supply pipes carry pressure all day and fight mineral deposits, soil chemistry and temperature swings. Galvanized steel rusts from the inside until the opening narrows. Older copper can develop pinholes where water chemistry and flow wear the wall thin.

The sign is rarely one big failure. It is a second pinhole six months after the first, or hot water that runs brown for a minute each morning.

Copper or PEX for the new lines

Copper

Rigid, time-tested, soldered joints.

  • Handles heat and sunlight well
  • Needs more cutting and openings in walls
  • Water chemistry can wear it over decades

PEX

Flexible plastic tubing with crimped fittings.

  • Snakes through walls with fewer holes
  • Resists scale and freezing splits better
  • Keep it out of direct sunlight and use proper fittings near the water heater

Neither is right for every house. The choice depends on access, the existing pipe, your plans for the walls, and what the inspector expects.

Pipe age and rules in Voorhees, NJ

Census ACS figures put about 1,100 of Voorhees' housing units in buildings put up before 1960, and the 1970s account for over 2,400 more. Pipe materials varied across those eras, so a house built in 1955 may still have galvanized steel inside, while a 1975 home is likelier to have copper.

On the utility side, a 2021 New Jersey law requires water utilities to replace lead and galvanized service lines by 2031, and New Jersey American Water says it will replace customer-owned lines found to be lead or galvanized. That covers the pipe from the street to the meter and house. See water line repair for that portion, while inside piping is the homeowner's side.

Permits and licensed work

New Jersey DCA guidance treats replacing existing piping with approved material of like capacity as minor work, which may begin once the enforcing agency is notified. Piping replaced to repair a leak is ordinary maintenance. Neighbors in Lindenwold and Gibbsboro follow their own boroughs' offices, so check locally. Anything that changes the layout, adds fixtures or alters capacity may be different.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Voorhees Construction Department. Plumbing must be done by licensed persons, and the permit steps should be planned alongside the pipe.

Insurance note. Homeowner insurance may cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe but often does not pay for the pipe replacement itself, so check your policy. When you compare repipe quotes, compare scope: what is replaced, what is reconnected, and what is left for you.

When not to repipe

If the leak is in one fitting and the pipe on either side is clean and thick, repair is the better value. If water pressure is low only on one floor, the cause may be a partly closed valve or an aerator, not the piping.

We will say so if a smaller repair does the job, and a leak locator can confirm before anything is opened.

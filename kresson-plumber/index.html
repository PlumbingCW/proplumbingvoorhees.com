About Kresson

Kresson is an unincorporated community in Voorhees Township. It was formerly known as Milford and is named for George Kress, who ran a general store in the early 1900s, according to a 2004 Philadelphia Inquirer reference. On a map it lies east of Echelon and Glendale.

Local landmarks include Kresson Elementary School, the public Kresson Golf Course and Kresson Lake. Kresson-Gibbsboro Road meets Cooper Road and Route 73 here. The township master plan places a Cluster Residential zoning district beside the golf course, so there are homes next to the open space.

The sources we reviewed give no fixed boundary for Kresson. This page therefore describes it by roads and landmarks rather than by a line on a map.

A basement water check before a big storm

General guidance for any house with a basement or crawlspace.

  1. 01

    Look at the pit

    If you have a sump pit, lift the lid and look for debris, the water level and the discharge pipe leaving the pit.

  2. 02

    Test the pump

    Pour a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should start, empty the pit and shut off. Stay out of standing water near electrical cords.

  3. 03

    Check the discharge

    Make sure the outlet pipe empties away from the foundation and is not crushed, frozen or blocked.

  4. 04

    Run the floor drain

    Pour water into any floor drain. A drain that backs up has a different cause from a wet wall.

  5. 05

    Write down what you see

    Note stains, smells and the date. A clear record helps a plumber tell groundwater from a pipe leak.

Watch it, or call a plumber

A damp basement is not always a plumbing problem, so a short wait can be reasonable.

Watch it for a day or two

For a damp patch with no sign of growth.

  • Dry the spot and mark its edge
  • Check again after rain and after a long shower
  • Look for a pipe that sweats or drips nearby

Call a plumber

For anything that grows or smells.

  • Water that returns after drying
  • A sewage smell or a gurgling floor drain
  • A pump that runs constantly or not at all

Groundwater, grading and gutters can also cause a wet basement. A plumber can help sort out which one is yours.

Services that fit a basement and a water heater

A pit that fills during storms needs a pump that actually runs, which is the point of sump pump installation and repair. If water seeps through walls even with a working pump, basement waterproofing is the longer-term conversation.

For a wet spot with no visible source, leak detection separates a pipe leak from groundwater. A tank that is failing is a water heater replacement question, and the basement check above is a good time to look at it.

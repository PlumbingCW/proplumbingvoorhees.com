Emergency Hydro Jetting in Voorhees, NJ

A sewer line that backs up again right after a cable visit needs more than another poke, and our 24/7 line is open when it does. Until we arrive, keep water use to a minimum and leave the basement drains uncovered so you can see if the level rises.

Hydro jetting is a drain-clearing method that sends water at high pressure through a hose and a nozzle, scouring grease, scale and root hair off the inside of the pipe. It cleans the whole wall instead of cutting a hole through the blockage.

Hydro jetting at a glance

What it does

Scours the pipe wall with pressurized water from a nozzle

Best for

Grease, scale, soap build-up, silt and root fibers

Not for

Collapsed, offset or badly cracked pipe

Camera first

We look inside before we pressurize the line

Where it works

Main sewer lines, branch lines, floor drain runs and commercial kitchens

Why a cable is sometimes not enough

A cable cutter spins through a clog and leaves a hole the size of its head. Grease and scale remain on the walls, and the opening narrows again, often within weeks. That is the pattern behind most of the repeat calls we hear about.

A jet nozzle fires water backward and sideways as well as forward. The stream pulls the hose along the pipe and strips the wall as it goes. Roots get cut back to the pipe wall instead of left as stubs that regrow.

Jetting versus cabling in plain terms

Cable machine

Fast and low cost for a single stoppage.

  • Punches a hole through the blockage
  • Leaves grease film in place
  • Fine for a first-time clog

Hydro jet

Longer to set up but cleans the full diameter.

  • Removes build-up along the wall
  • Suits kitchen lines that clog yearly
  • Needs a sound pipe to be safe

Where a line has already been cabled and failed, we usually move to the jet. Where a pipe is old and thin, we skip it, and the next section explains why.

Older Voorhees houses and the pipes under them

According to Census survey data, more than 1,100 houses in Voorhees were built before 1960, including 378 from 1939 or earlier. Pipe laid in those decades can be cast iron, clay or fiber conduit, and the interior wall may be flaky from age.

That is why we run a camera before any jetting on a pre-1960 house. If the wall is scaled but sound, a jet does the job. If it is cracked or sagging, pressure could widen the damage, and a repair plan comes first.

Soon

How a jetting visit runs

  1. 01

    Camera look

    A small camera on a flexible rod shows the pipe condition and the position of the blockage.

  2. 02

    Set up at the cleanout

    We thread the hose through the cleanout, with the nozzle sized to the pipe diameter.

  3. 03

    Jet in passes

    We work forward and pull back, adjusting pressure so we do not stress joints.

  4. 04

    Confirm with a second pass

    A follow-up camera run or a flow test shows the wall is clean and the water drains.

When not to jet

If the camera shows a collapsed section, a belly that holds water or a joint that has separated, a jet will not fix it and may spread debris. We would rather tell you that and move you to sewer line repair than clean a pipe that is failing.

A leak under the kitchen sink right now

A garbage disposal is a grinder bolted to the bottom of the sink drain, with a motor, a grind chamber, a mounting ring, and a drain outlet that connects to the trap. Leaks come from three places: the sink flange at the top, the body seams, or the drain outlet and dishwasher hose.

Water in the cabinet can swell the base panel within a day, so treat a steady leak as urgent.

  • 1Switch the disposal off and unplug it under the sink, or flip its breaker if it is hardwired
  • 2Close the two shutoffs under the sink if the faucet is involved, then stop using the sink
  • 3Empty the cabinet and set a bowl under the drip, noting where it comes from
  • 4Call (855) 575-2890 and say whether it drips from the top, the side or the pipe on the right

What to do before we arrive

  • ✓Cut the power first. Switch the disposal off at the wall switch, then unplug it under the sink or turn off its breaker.
  • ✓Keep hands out of the chamber. Never reach into a disposal. Use tongs or pliers for anything you can see, and only with the power off.
  • ✓Stop running water into it. A leaking or jammed unit will overflow the sink or drip below. Set a bucket under the trap and empty the cabinet.
  • ✓Note what you heard. Humming, silence or a grinding sound each point to a different fault, and the description speeds up the repair.

A humming or silent disposal, step by step

A jam is the most common fault, and usually the safest to clear. Power stays off the whole time.

  1. 01

    Cut power

    Turn off the wall switch, then unplug the unit or throw its breaker.

  2. 02

    Rotate the flywheel

    Insert a hex key in the bottom socket and work it back and forth until the plates move freely.

  3. 03

    Remove the obstruction

    Use tongs or pliers from above. Common culprits are olive pits, bottle caps, utensils and fruit stones.

  4. 04

    Press reset

    The red button on the bottom pops when the motor overheats. Wait a few minutes, then press it.

  5. 05

    Test with water

    Restore power, run cold water and flip the switch. If it only hums, stop and call.

If the unit does not respond after a reset, the motor or its wiring is likely worn, and repair at that point rarely makes economic sense.

Fix it or swap in a new one

A good rule: leaks from the unit body and dead motors lead to replacement, while jams and loose connections lead to repair.

Repairable

The unit itself is sound.

  • Jammed flywheel
  • Leaking sink flange (new putty and mounting ring)
  • Loose drain outlet or dishwasher hose
  • Tripped reset

Replace

Not worth sinking money into.

  • Seam or body leak (casing corroded)
  • Motor that hums but never turns after a clear jam
  • Rusted grind chamber
  • A unit that trips the breaker repeatedly

A replacement unit is usually a sized swap on the existing mount. The longer it has been in service, the more likely replacement is the honest answer.

When the problem is the drain, not the disposal

A kitchen sink drains into a line that also carries grease, coffee grounds and starchy foods like rice and pasta. A disposal grinds them fine, but fine is not the same as gone, and they settle in the trap and the first stretch of drain pipe.

When both sink bowls fill together or the water rises while the dishwasher drains, the blockage is past the disposal. A professional drain cleaning clears it, and for a repeat clog a camera shows how far the build-up runs.

Removing and reinstalling a disposal

Customers often ask for a reinstall after a cabinet repair, a new sink or a countertop swap. The unit is unmounted by turning its ring, the drain is disconnected, and the dishwasher hose is freed.

On reinstall we replace the plumber's putty or gasket under the sink flange, knock out the dishwasher plug if a hose is being added, and reconnect to the trap. Wiring is either a plug or a hardwired connection, and electrical work belongs to a licensed electrician.

What changes the price of a disposal job

Repair or replacement

A flange reseal is a lighter job than a new unit.

Horsepower and build

Larger motors and stainless chambers cost more than basic models.

Mounting system

Some brands require a matching mount or a new bracket.

Electrical setup

A missing outlet or switch adds electrical work.

Trap and drain condition

Corroded pipes or an old trap often get replaced at the same time.

Insurance note. Homeowner insurance may cover water damage from a sudden leak but usually not the disposal itself, so check your policy. You see the price before work starts, with repair and replacement priced side by side where both make sense.

Keeping a disposal running

  • ✓Run cold water while grinding and for 15 seconds after
  • ✓Skip grease, bones, fibrous peels, coffee grounds and pasta
  • ✓Grind ice and citrus peel occasionally to freshen the chamber
  • ✓Check under the sink every few months for dampness or rust
  • ✓Replace the unit before it starts leaking if it is old and noisy

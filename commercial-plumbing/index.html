Water on the floor of your business right now

Commercial plumbing is the repair, maintenance and installation of the water, waste and venting systems in non-residential buildings, from a single retail unit to a multi-tenant office or an apartment complex. The stakes differ from a house because one failure can shut a kitchen, close a restroom or damage a neighbor's suite.

A floor drain that is overflowing, a restroom backing up or a supply line spraying all call for the same first minutes of control.

  • 1Stop the sourceClose the fixture stop, the suite shutoff or the building main. Know where these are before the day you need them.
  • 2Cut power near waterIf water is reaching outlets, panels, coolers or a lift pump, turn off power from a dry spot or leave it to the utility and electricians.
  • 3Protect what can be movedLift inventory, files and equipment off the floor. Photograph the water and the damage before cleanup for your records.
  • 4Call the 24/7 lineCall (855) 575-2890 and describe the fixture, the floor, and whether sewage is involved. That decides what we bring.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. Plumbing waits until the building is cleared.

Finding a hidden leak without opening every wall

Commercial leaks are often found late: a stained ceiling tile, a damp carpet edge, a water bill that climbed. We narrow them with a meter check (all fixtures off, does the dial still move), pressure testing of isolated branches, acoustic listening and thermal imaging of walls and slabs.

Tracing a leak this way keeps demolition small. Opening one section of drywall is a better trade than opening a corridor.

If the leak is in a tenant space above yours, the evidence we gather helps the building manager and insurers sort out responsibility.

Preventive habits for managers

Label every shutoff valve and keep a map where staff can find it. Exercise valves twice a year so they turn when needed.

Schedule camera inspection and cleaning of floor drains and the main line in kitchens and clinics, and keep grease and wipes out of drains. Where a building's drainage is old, a sewer camera inspection shows the pipe condition before a backup does.

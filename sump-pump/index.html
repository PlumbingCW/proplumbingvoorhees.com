Emergency Sump Pump Repair in Voorhees, NJ

A sump pump sits in a pit in the lowest part of a basement or crawlspace and pushes groundwater out and away before it reaches the floor. If yours has quit during a storm in Voorhees, our 24/7 line is open, and while you wait you should move belongings off the floor, stay out of standing water near outlets and switch off power to the area if water is rising.

Most failures come down to a stuck float, a dead motor, a blocked discharge or a power outage. We repair, replace and add backup pumps.

Basement flooding because the pump stopped

A pit that stops moving water can fill a basement within hours during heavy rain. The goal is to slow the damage and stay safe until a technician arrives.

  • 1Check power firstLook at the breaker and the GFCI outlet. A tripped outlet is a common cause. Do not touch anything while standing in water.
  • 2Lift the floatIf the float is stuck against the pit wall, free it. The pump should start, and then you know it is a jam.
  • 3Check the dischargeMake sure the outdoor outlet is clear and not frozen or buried.
  • 4Bail or use a wet vacuumKeep the pit level low while you wait. Use a bucket, not an extension cord in wet areas.
  • 5Call the 24/7 lineAsk for a same-day or after-hours visit.

If water has reached outlets or the panel, leave the room and call the electric utility or an electrician. Do not wade in.

Pump types and backups

The right system depends on how often your pit fills and what happens during outages.

Submersible primary pump

Sits inside the pit, quiet and common for homes.

  • Handles most basements
  • Needs a check valve
  • Fails when the float sticks

Pedestal pump

Motor sits above the pit, easier to service.

  • Shorter life in hot, damp rooms
  • Louder
  • Needs a narrow pit

Battery or water-powered backup

A second pump for outages and primary failure.

  • Works during blackouts
  • Batteries need replacement
  • Water-powered types need adequate water pressure

Testing a pump before the storm

  1. 01

    Pour in a bucket of water

    The float should rise and the pump should start and empty the pit.

  2. 02

    Listen and watch

    Grinding, rattling or a motor that runs without moving water means trouble.

  3. 03

    Check the check valve

    Water should not slap back into the pit after the pump stops.

  4. 04

    Look outside

    Follow the discharge pipe and make sure it drains away from the foundation.

  5. 05

    Test the backup

    Unplug the primary briefly and confirm the backup starts.

Sump water must not be sent where it could harm the sewer system or a neighbor's property. The township holds the rules, so ask the Voorhees Construction Department before you choose a discharge path.

When NOT to install a new pump

If the water comes from a burst supply pipe, a failed water heater or a sewer backup, a sump pump will not solve it. Our leak detection team can locate the real source before you spend money.

What sets the price of a sump pump job

Pump type and horsepower

Higher capacity pumps cost more but handle heavier flows.

Pit condition

A cracked or undersized pit may need replacing.

Discharge routing

A longer run, new pipe or burying a line adds labor. Call 811 before digging outside.

Backup system

A battery or water-powered backup adds equipment and setup.

Time of call

Overnight and weekend dispatch is priced differently from scheduled work.

Insurance note. Homeowner insurance may exclude groundwater and seepage unless you added a sump or water backup endorsement, so check your policy. Get a clear price before work starts and ask what is included for the check valve and discharge pipe.
